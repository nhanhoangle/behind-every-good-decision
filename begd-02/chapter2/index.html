What is
analytics?

Analytics is a structured way to solve a business problem with data and analysis, so that the outcome is an action with real impact—not merely an interesting report.

In this chapterDefinition
Decision anatomy
Questions first

A working definition

Business analytics joins three elements: a meaningful question, evidence that speaks to it, and a decision someone can take. Without a question, data is noise. Without action, an answer is unfinished.

The formula

Business question + relevant data + useful analysis = insight that drives action.

Anatomy of a good decision

Begin by identifying the decision owner and the outcome they want to improve. Then make the question specific enough that everyone can describe what will be measured, compared, or predicted.

QUESTIONWhat choice must we make?
CONTEXTWhy does this choice matter now?
EVIDENCEWhat might change our answer?
ACTIONWhat will we do when we know more?

Questions first, tools second

Starting with a dashboard or large dataset is tempting. A stronger approach starts with the business question; it then becomes much easier to choose the right data and method.

From vague to useful

Vague: Why are sales down?
Useful: Which customer segments and channels account for the decline in repeat sales this quarter, and which intervention is most likely to reverse it?
