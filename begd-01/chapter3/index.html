Seven lenses for a business question

Each methodology is a different way to frame evidence. They can be used alone or combined as an investigation becomes more specific.

01Aggregate analysis

Describe a group or compare its parts: Which region, product, or customer group performs best?

02Correlation analysis

Look for relationships: Which behaviors tend to occur together?

03Trends analysis

Follow change over time: When did performance shift, and how fast?

04Sizing & estimation

Estimate an opportunity when complete historical data is unavailable.

05Predictive / time series

Use past patterns to anticipate what is likely to happen next.

06Segmentation

Group similar customers, products, or situations to tailor decisions.

07Customer life cycle

Examine the stages from awareness to purchase, retention, and return.

How to choose the method

Match the method to the question before touching the data. If the question asks “what happened?” start with aggregate or trend analysis. If it asks “what might happen?” a predictive approach may help. If it asks “for whom?” segmentation often reveals the most useful answer.

One question, several lenses

A wine retailer wants to increase repeat orders. Aggregate analysis identifies the strongest customer group; trends show whether repeat ordering is improving; segmentation exposes different buying patterns; lifecycle analysis locates the point where customers disengage. Each lens contributes to a more actionable recommendation.

Start simply

Simple methods are powerful because they are explainable, fast to test, and easy to connect to a decision. Begin with the least complex technique that answers the question. Escalate only when the decision truly needs more precision.

Chapter takeaway

The best methodology is not the most sophisticated one. It is the one that gives the decision-maker a reliable next move.
