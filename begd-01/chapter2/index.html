What is
analytics?

Analytics is a structured method for solving a business problem with data and analysis—so that the result is an action with impact, not simply an interesting report.

Anatomy of a decision
Questions first

A working definition

Business analytics connects three things: a meaningful question, evidence that bears on it, and a decision someone can take. Leaving out any one of them weakens the work. Data without a question is noise; an answer without action is unfinished.

The formula

Business question + relevant data + useful analysis = insight that can drive action.

Anatomy of a good decision

A clear analysis begins by defining the decision owner and the outcome they are trying to improve. It then narrows the question until the team can explain what it will measure, compare, or predict.

QUESTIONWhat choice must we make?
CONTEXTWhat makes this choice important now?
EVIDENCEWhat information could change the answer?
ACTIONWhat will we do when we know more?

This structure keeps analysis practical. It prevents teams from spending weeks exploring data that has no bearing on the decision at hand.

Questions first, tools second

It is tempting to begin with the latest dashboard or the largest available data set. The stronger sequence begins with the business question. Once the question is clear, the suitable data and method are usually much easier to identify.

From vague to useful

Vague: “Why are sales down?”
Useful: “Which customer segments and channels account for the decline in repeat sales during the past quarter, and which intervention is most likely to reverse it?”

The second question gives analysis a boundary, identifies comparison groups, and points toward a decision. That is the difference between reporting and analytics.
